GearUp — Usability Study

Two rounds of moderated usability testing on a gaming eCommerce app prototype. Identified critical friction points across navigation, accessibility, and checkout, then validated design changes with a second round. SUS improved from 83 (Grade B) to 93.5 (Grade A).

02

Measuring the Limits of Immersion

A controlled VR experiment examining how sensorimotor feedback error and camera perspective shift affect objective task performance. Found tracking fidelity produced a ~5× larger accuracy cost than viewpoint distortion — a concrete signal for where VR engineering budgets should go.

03

Diagnosing Curriculum Friction

Two years of statistics course gradebook data analyzed to find where students consistently lose ground — without asking them directly. Chapter 2 averaged 81.8% against 89.8% across all other chapters, held up under repeated-measures ANOVA and a mixed-effects robustness check, and sat at the bottom in 4 of 5 course sections.

About

I'm a researcher with a background in cognitive psychology, experimental neuroscience, and statistics. My PhD work used virtual reality and fMRI to study how the brain constructs a sense of body ownership and spatial presence — questions that turn out to matter a great deal when you're designing immersive digital environments.

I came to UX research because the questions are the same ones I've always worked on: how do people make sense of the systems they're embedded in, and where does that process break down? The methods I use are quantitative by default: controlled experiments, behavioral outcome data, mixed-effects models. I think the most useful research finding is one you can act on with confidence.

I'm currently an Assistant Professor of Psychology at LaGuardia Community College (CUNY), where I also apply these methods to understanding how students learn.
